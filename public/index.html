CHAPTER ONE · CORNWALL, 1985

The blue Ford Cortina was weighed down to its axles, the boot tied shut with hairy garden twine. Dad was at the wheel with that quiet, stubborn determination of his, refusing point-blank to touch the motorway because ‘the old coaching roads had more character.’ Halfway up Haldon Hill near Exeter, the radiator gave up with an indignant hiss of steam, stranding us on a patch of sun-baked grass while Mum distributed cheese and pickle sandwiches wrapped in warm foil.

Yet none of us minded. By seven that evening, the road curved over the headland and there was the Atlantic — grey, vast, and welcoming. The cottage smelled of sea-salt and damp wool, but waking up the next morning to the screech of gulls outside our window, I knew that no five-star hotel could ever match it.
